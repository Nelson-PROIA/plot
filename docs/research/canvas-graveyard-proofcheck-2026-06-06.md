# Verdict: Is graph/canvas-based code review doomed, or viable?

Only **Haystack** is a genuine "the visual interface was too alien for review" data point — and even they kept the door open to visualization in editors, just not for review.

## 2. The living counter-case (visual tools alive/growing in 2026)

| Tool | Status | Is the visual the PRIMARY surface or a COMPLEMENT? |
|---|---|---|
| **CodeViz** (codeviz.ai) | Alive, YC S24, self-reports 70k+ devs (unaudited) | **PRIMARY.** The C4/UML interactive map *is* the product ("Code architecture in one clear view"). The single strongest rebuttal to "visual-first kills the product." |
| **GitDiagram** (gitdiagram.com) | Alive, indie, ~2.7k stars | **PRIMARY** — repo→diagram is the whole tool. Real standing demand, but indie scale. |
| **CodeRabbit Atlas** | Alive & fast-growing (~$40M ARR est., $60M Series B, 8k+ paying customers) | **COMPLEMENT.** Diagrams are selective — their own words: *"Not every layer gets one."* The diff stays central. Growth is **not** attributable to the visual surface. |
| **Entelligence** | Alive (no public traction numbers) | **COMPLEMENT.** Sequence/architecture diagrams included *"when needed."* |
| **DeepWiki** (Cognition) | Alive, 50k+ repos / 4B+ LOC | **COMPLEMENT.** Primary surfaces are AI prose docs + chat (*"documentation you can talk to"*); diagrams embedded. |
| **AppMap** | Alive, active commits 2026 | **Largely primary**, but now heavily paired with its Navie AI chat. |
| **CodeScene** | Alive, enterprise ($8.3M Series A 2023), steady/niche | **Largely primary** (hotspot/knowledge maps) but not high-growth. |
| **Greptile / FalkorDB / code-review-graph** | Alive, growing (Greptile 1,000+ teams) | **NOT user-facing visual.** The graph is an **internal retrieval engine** powering text/AI review. |
| **Sourcegraph** (cautionary) | Alive but **retreated** from visual code-intelligence to enterprise RAG; killed Cody Free/Pro 2025 | The former code-graph standard-bearer concluded the **visual surface was not the growth driver**. |

**The dominant 2026 pattern:** the **graph powers the AI under the hood; the human-facing surface stays text/diff/chat with diagrams as selective complements.** The two clear "visual-first AND alive" exceptions are CodeViz (real scale) and GitDiagram (indie). No fast-growing *review* product makes the visual its primary surface.

## 3. What the science says (honestly, both ways)

**Evidence FOR visualization:**
- **REACHER (LaToza & Myers, VL/HCC 2011)** — the strongest *objective* result. Interactive call-graph viz vs. Eclipse, 12 participants on reachability questions: *"participants with REACHER were over 5 times more successful in significantly less time"* (7.2 vs 11.1 min). **Causal**, but **narrow** — control-flow reachability, a task where graphs have a natural edge.
- **CodeMap (arXiv 2504.04553)** — users read LLM text 79% less and viewed maps more, rated map UX higher. **But subjective only** — it measured *perceptions*, not comprehension accuracy. Shows preference/behavior shift, **not** proof of better understanding.
- **Citation-Grounded (arXiv 2512.12117)** — 92% citation accuracy, 62% of architectural queries benefited from cross-file evidence text-only missed. **But this is graph-as-RETRIEVAL for an LLM (RAG), not a human-facing visualization at all.** Supports the graph *data model*, not the visual UI.

**Evidence AGAINST / null:**
- **di Biase & Bacchelli (PeerJ CS, n=28), the most rigorous review experiment** — better-decomposed changes *"leads to fewer wrongly reported issues"* and more context-seeking, **yet** *"impacts neither understanding the change rationale nor the number of found defects."* Structuring reduces false positives but did **not** increase bugs found or comprehension.
- **Baum et al. (ICSME 2017)** — reviewers *want* relatedness-based grouping over alphabetical, but it's theory-building (interviews/survey), not a proven outcome gain.
- **Software-viz SLRs** — ~62% of viz approaches lack rigorous evaluation; *"low adoption... results from their unproved effectiveness."* UML adoption has **declined**.
- **Petre (CACM 1995)** — graphics aren't automatically clearer than text; comprehensibility lives in learned "secondary notation," and *"less experienced users appear unable to exploit (or even notice) the graphical cues that might help them."*

**Balanced read:** Graph/spatial structure reliably helps **navigation and specific relational queries** (reachability, cross-file dependencies) and improves the **retrieval data model**, and is reliably **preferred** by users. Rigorous proof that a visual UI causes **better general comprehension or more defects caught** is **weak-to-null**, and effectiveness depends on **learned reading skills** — it can actively fail novices.

## 5. Honest bottom line for the founder

Graph-based code review is **conditional, not doomed — but the specific bet "review *on* an infinite canvas with dependency arrows" is the riskiest version of it, and it's the only variant with a documented user-rejection** (Haystack's founder, after testing with many engineers: *"too confusing... how alien the interface is"*, plus a user asking *"what do those groups and arrows mean?"*). Crucially, **none of the dead products died because of their visual UI** — CodeSee, Sourcetrail, and Pierre all died of monetization/strategy with the visuals actively *loved*, so "canvas products all died" is false as stated; they died as *businesses*. The science backs you **narrowly**: interactive graphs measurably win for navigation and reachability/cross-file questions (REACHER, 5x), users reliably *prefer* maps (CodeMap), and the graph is a superb *retrieval* substrate (the 2026 winners all use it under the hood) — but rigorous proof that a visual surface improves general comprehension or catches more bugs is weak-to-null, and graphs can lose novices who lack "readership skills." The market's revealed answer in 2026 is unambiguous: **every fast-growing review tool (CodeRabbit, Devin, Stage, cubic, Graphite) made ordered/grouped diffs or a narrated walkthrough the primary surface and kept diagrams as a selective complement — "not every layer gets one"** — while pure visual-first survives mainly in *comprehension/architecture* tools (CodeViz, GitDiagram, DeepWiki), not in *review*. So the conditions the evidence actually supports: (a) use the graph as the engine and an *optional, on-demand* view, not the mandatory review surface; (b) default to a linear, ordered, explained reading path with the graph one click away for the relational questions where it genuinely wins; (c) if you keep the canvas central, you must beat the "alien interface" problem with onboarding and progressive disclosure that Haystack didn't solve. Your instinct that "reviewing on a graph is the key winner" is defensible **as a differentiating complement and as your internal model of the change**, but the evidence does not support making the raw graph the thing users are forced to review *in* — make it earnable, not mandatory."
